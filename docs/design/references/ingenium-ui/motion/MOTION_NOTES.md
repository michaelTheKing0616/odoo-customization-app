# Ingenium motion references

Official clips pulled from vendors' live pages on 2026-10-21. Each one is trimmed to its most active window of 14 s or less, encoded as H.264 at up to 1080p and 30 fps, with no audio.

**How these were measured.** Each clip was decoded at 30 fps and 96 px wide in grayscale. I computed the mean frame-to-frame difference, which gives a motion-energy curve. A "motion burst" is a run of frames above an adaptive threshold that lasts 133 ms to 2 s. The burst durations are measured to the nearest 33 ms frame. They are only as accurate as the vendors' edits, since marketing videos may be sped up or cut. The easing character is a heuristic based on where the burst's energy peaks: an early peak means ease-out, a mid peak means ease-in-out, and a second peak means possible overshoot. It is not a fitted curve, so confirm it by eye before copying values. The "what happens" and "why it feels good" columns are my reading of the source title and low-res keyframes, so they are editorial, not measured. The post date is the CDN's Last-Modified header, which is a proxy for when the file was uploaded, unless a changelog date is given.
